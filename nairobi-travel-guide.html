Nairobi at a glance

Nairobi is East Africa's most important city. The region's financial, diplomatic, tech and cultural hub. At 1,795 metres above sea level, it has a year-round temperate climate (18–26°C) that is the envy of tropical capitals. It is home to the UN Environment Programme and UN-Habitat headquarters, Africa's most active startup ecosystem (dubbed "Silicon Savannah"), and the only capital city in the world with a national park on its doorstep.

Population: approximately 5 million in the city, 15 million in Greater Nairobi. Languages: English (official), Swahili (official), and dozens of community languages. Currency: Kenya Shilling (KES). Time zone: EAT (UTC+3).

Best areas to stay in Nairobi

Nairobi's best short-stay areas each have a distinct character:

  • Kilimani: the most popular short-stay neighbourhood. Yaya Centre, Junction Mall, excellent restaurants. KES 3,000–8,000/night.
  • Westlands: business and nightlife hub. Sarit Centre, the best Nairobi bars and restaurants. KES 3,500–10,000/night.
  • Karen: leafy, exclusive, villas and gardens. Karen Blixen Museum, Giraffe Centre. KES 5,000–20,000/night.
  • Lavington: quiet, tree-lined, embassy belt. Best Indian food in Nairobi. KES 4,000–9,000/night.
  • Gigiri: UN complex, US Embassy, Village Market. Best for diplomatic and NGO visitors. KES 5,000–15,000/night.
  • Upper Hill: corporate district, hospitals, conference venues. KES 4,500–11,000/night.
  • Kileleshwa: quiet mid-city, modern blocks, good value. KES 3,500–8,500/night.

Top things to do in Nairobi

Wildlife & Nature

  • Nairobi National Park: the world's only national park inside a capital city. Lions, rhino, giraffe, zebra and over 400 bird species, 10 km from the CBD. Entry from KES 3,000 for residents, USD 60 for non-residents.
  • Giraffe Centre: hand-feed endangered Rothschild giraffes. Karen. KES 1,500 adults.
  • David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust: baby elephant orphanage. Langata. KES 500 adults. Book visits in advance.
  • Karura Forest: 1,000+ hectare urban forest. Trails, waterfalls, cycling. Walking free; cycling KES 600.
  • City Park: colonial-era park in Parklands. Large troops of Sykes' monkeys. Free.

Culture & Museums

  • Nairobi National Museum: Kenya's most important museum. Natural history, cultural exhibits, prehistoric fossils. Museum Hill.
  • Karen Blixen Museum: the farm that inspired Out of Africa. The original farmhouse with original furniture. Karen suburb.
  • Bomas of Kenya: cultural centre with traditional dances and village replicas. Langata.
  • Railway Museum: Uganda Railway history, vintage engines. Near Nairobi station.

Day Trips from Nairobi

  • Lake Naivasha: Hell's Gate National Park (cycling safari), Crescent Island walking safari. 90 minutes.
  • Lake Nakuru: flamingos, white rhino, 400+ birds. 2 hours.
  • Masai Mara: the world's greatest wildlife destination. 5.5 hours by road or 45 minutes by air from Wilson Airport.
  • Nanyuki/Ol Pejeta: last northern white rhinos, Mount Kenya. 2.5 hours.

Where to eat in Nairobi

Nairobi has East Africa's most sophisticated food scene. A reflection of the city's extraordinary diversity.

Kenyan cuisine

  • Nyama Choma: roast meat, typically goat, the national staple. Best at Carnivore (Langata) or local joints throughout the city.
  • Githeri: maize and bean stew. Classic Kenyan home food.
  • Ugali: maize flour staple, served with sukuma wiki (kale), beef stew or fish.
  • Mandazi: Swahili doughnuts. Eaten at breakfast with chai (spiced tea).

Best restaurants by area

  • Westlands: Artcaffe (best coffee), Tamarind (seafood), Fogo Gaucho (Brazilian steakhouse)
  • Kilimani: The Junction restaurant strip, Pizza Mojo, great Indian options
  • Karen: Karen Blixen Coffee Garden, Talisman, The Cultured Goat
  • Parklands: Outstanding Indian. Havyaka, local Gujarati places along Limuru Road

Getting around Nairobi

  • Uber / Bolt: safest, most reliable option. Always use apps over roadside taxis. Nairobi price: KES 200–600 for most central journeys.
  • Matatus: Nairobi's minibus network. Cheap (KES 30–100) but chaotic. Routes numbered; locals use them daily. Not recommended for first-time visitors.
  • Nairobi Expressway: the 27.1 km M-Pesa-paid toll road connecting Mlolongo/JKIA to Westlands. Fastest route; heavily cuts traffic. KES 100–300 depending on section.
  • SGR (Standard Gauge Railway): from Nairobi's Central Station to Syokimau (for JKIA area) and onward to Mombasa (4.5 hours). Clean, punctual, excellent value.

Safety in Nairobi

Nairobi has a reputation that often exceeds its reality in established tourist areas. The key rules:

  • Always use Uber or Bolt, never roadside taxis
  • Don't walk in unfamiliar areas after dark
  • Keep phones out of sight on streets, particularly in the CBD
  • Westlands, Kilimani, Karen, Gigiri and Upper Hill are genuinely safe during daylight
  • Nairobi National Park, Karura Forest and the Giraffe Centre are all extremely safe

Most visitors to Nairobi who stay in established areas and use Uber/Bolt have completely incident-free visits. The city is significantly safer than its reputation suggests for well-prepared travellers.

Practical information

  • Visa: Most nationalities can get a Kenya eVisa at evisa.go.ke (USD 50 for most nationals). East Africa Tourist Visa covers Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda (USD 100). Apply in advance.
  • Currency: Kenya Shilling (KES). Forex bureaux give better rates than banks. M-Pesa works for almost everything local. Cards widely accepted in Westlands/Karen/Kilimani.
  • SIM card: Safaricom (best coverage), Airtel. Buy at JKIA or any supermarket. Safaricom data is reliable throughout Nairobi.
  • JKIA Airport: 18 km from Westlands. 20–40 minutes by car or Expressway depending on traffic. Uber available from arrivals.
  • Wilson Airport: 8 km from CBD. For domestic and East African safari flights (Safarilink, Airkenya).
  • Best time to visit: January–March and June–October are best. Long rains April–May; short rains November. Nairobi's altitude means it's never oppressively hot.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nairobi safe for tourists?
Yes, for well-prepared visitors staying in established areas (Westlands, Kilimani, Karen, Gigiri). Use Uber/Bolt exclusively, don't display valuables on streets, and avoid unfamiliar areas after dark. Most visitors have completely incident-free experiences.
What is the best area to stay in Nairobi?
Kilimani for walkability and restaurants; Westlands for nightlife and business; Karen for space and exclusivity; Gigiri for UN/diplomatic business; Upper Hill for hospitals and conferences. Browse zero-commission stays on Cabana.
What currency do they use in Nairobi?
The Kenya Shilling (KES). M-Pesa mobile money is dominant for local transactions. Major card brands accepted widely in shopping areas. Forex bureaux give better exchange rates than bank branches.
How do I get from JKIA to Nairobi city?
Uber or Bolt from arrivals (KES 800–1,500 to most central areas). The Nairobi Expressway makes this 20–30 minutes in normal traffic. Avoid unlicensed taxi touts at the airport, always use the app.
What is the best time to visit Nairobi?
January–March (dry, warm) and June–October (dry, slightly cooler, great for safari) are optimal. April–May are long rains. Green and less busy. Nairobi's altitude (1,795m) keeps temperatures pleasant year-round: 18–26°C.
