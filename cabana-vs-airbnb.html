Commission: the fundamental difference

This is the entire ballgame. Everything else is secondary.

PlatformHost commissionGuest service feeTotal platform take
Cabana0%0%0%
Airbnb3% host-only fee*6–15% guest fee9–18% total

*Airbnb's "split" fee model charges hosts 3% + guests up to 15%. The older "host-only" model charges hosts up to 20% with no guest fee. Many African hosts are on the host-only model. Check your listing settings.

On a KES 10,000/night Nairobi apartment:

  • On Cabana: Host receives KES 10,000. Guest pays KES 10,000.
  • On Airbnb (split model): Host receives KES 9,700. Guest pays KES 11,200–11,500.
  • On Airbnb (host-only model): Host receives KES 8,000–8,500. Guest pays KES 10,000.

Over 30 booked nights at KES 10,000: Airbnb takes KES 15,000–60,000 from a Nairobi host. Cabana takes KES 0.

What guests actually pay: the checkout surprise

Airbnb's biggest guest complaint is the checkout surprise. You find a KES 8,000/night apartment, it looks perfect, you get to payment and suddenly it's KES 11,500/night after the service fee and cleaning fee. This is not a minor quirk. It is by design.

On Cabana: the price shown is the price you pay. No service fee. No cleaning fee added at checkout (hosts may include it in their listed rate if they choose, but it is disclosed upfront). No booking surcharge. The guest checkout on Cabana is exactly what the listing shows.

For hosts: which platform earns more?

This is simple arithmetic:

A Nairobi host with a Kilimani 1-bedroom at KES 6,000/night, booking 20 nights/month:

  • On Airbnb (split model): KES 6,000 × 20 × 0.97 = KES 116,400/month
  • On Airbnb (host-only model): KES 6,000 × 20 × 0.80 = KES 96,000/month
  • On Cabana: KES 6,000 × 20 × 1.00 = KES 120,000/month

Cabana hosts earn more. Always. Because zero commission means 100% retention.

The verdict

For African travel, whether you are a guest or a host. Cabana wins on every measurable dimension that matters: commission (0% vs up to 18%), payment methods (M-Pesa vs card-only), African-built expertise, and feature set (Cabana Match, APA, flights, safaris. None available on Airbnb).

The one area where Airbnb has an advantage is global brand recognition and existing listing volume. More travellers globally know Airbnb than Cabana. This changes as Cabana grows, and the zero-commission model means that growth is structurally inevitable as African hosts recognise the difference.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cabana better than Airbnb in Africa?
Yes, for African accommodation. Cabana charges zero commission (Airbnb takes up to 18% combined). Cabana supports M-Pesa (Airbnb does not). Cabana is built for Africa (Airbnb is built in San Francisco). Cabana Match and APA AI are available nowhere on Airbnb. For African hosts and guests, Cabana is the better platform.
How much does Airbnb charge in Kenya?
Airbnb charges Kenyan hosts either 3% (split fee model) or up to 20% (host-only model) per booking. Guests pay an additional 6–15% service fee. This means Airbnb takes 9–18% of every Kenyan booking. Cabana charges 0%.
Does Airbnb accept M-Pesa in Kenya?
No. Airbnb does not support M-Pesa payments in Kenya. Guests must pay by international credit or debit card. Cabana is the only major booking platform with native M-Pesa support for Kenya accommodation.
Can I list on both Cabana and Airbnb?
Yes. Many hosts list on both platforms. Cabana is free to list on with zero commission. Airbnb charges commission per booking. Hosts typically prefer Cabana for direct enquiries (zero commission) and use Airbnb for global visibility while accepting the commission cost.
