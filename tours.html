Free walking tours in Nairobi with local guides

Discover Nairobi on foot with the city's best-loved walking tours, powered by GuruWalk. Led by knowledgeable local guides, these free walking tours take you through the heart of Nairobi — from the historic Parliament Buildings, KICC and Jamia Mosque to the bustling City Market and Maasai Market. Free tours run on a pay-what-you-want model, so you decide what the experience was worth at the end. With hundreds of five-star reviews, these are among the highest-rated experiences in the city.

Beyond the city centre, you can join immersive community tours of Kibera, one of East Africa's largest urban settlements, or escape into nature with a guided walk through Karura Forest, home to waterfalls, Mau Mau caves and a canopy walk. For the outdoors enthusiast, we also feature hiking and cycling trails near Nairobi via komoot — from the Ngong Hills and Karura Forest loops to Hell Gate and Mount Kenya.

Types of tours in Nairobi

Whether you love history, culture, community stories or the outdoors, there's a Nairobi tour for you. City walks cover the colonial and independence history of Kenya, its 43 tribes and traditions. Community tours offer an honest, respectful look at everyday life. Nature walks and trails let you experience Kenya's landscapes just minutes from the city.

Frequently asked questions

Are the walking tours in Nairobi really free?

Yes — free walking tours operate on a pay-what-you-want basis. There's no fixed price. At the end of the tour, you tip your guide whatever you feel the experience was worth. Some nature tours have a small park entry fee.

How do I book a Nairobi walking tour?

Tap any tour on this page to view full details and book directly on GuruWalk. You'll see the meeting point, duration, guide name, languages and reviews before you confirm.

Is it safe to tour Kibera?

Yes — the community tours are led by local guides who live in the area, are designed to be respectful and educational, and directly support community projects. They go far beyond stereotypes to show real everyday life.
