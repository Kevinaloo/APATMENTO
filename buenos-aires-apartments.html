Buenos Aires: South America's most sophisticated city

Buenos Aires is the most European city in South America. Founded by Spanish settlers and reshaped by Italian and other European immigration in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The wide boulevards, café culture, opera houses and bookshops (El Ateneo Grand Splendid. The world's most beautiful bookshop) tell this story.

Palermo: BA's most vibrant neighbourhood. Palermo Soho (boutiques, restaurants), Palermo Hollywood (media, nightlife), the parks.
San Telmo: the oldest neighbourhood. Sunday antique market, tango milongas, cobblestone streets.
Recoleta: elegant, upscale. The famous Recoleta Cemetery (Eva Perón's tomb), the MALBA art museum.
Villa Crespo: the new Palermo. Better value, excellent restaurants, up-and-coming.
La Boca: the colourful Caminito, the Boca Juniors stadium (La Bombonera). Touristy but visually extraordinary.

Getting to Buenos Aires

Ezeiza International Airport (EZE) is 35 km from the city centre, about 45–60 minutes by taxi or Bus Aeropuerto. The city also has the domestic Aeroparque (AEP) 3 km from the centre for regional flights.

Buenos Aires: frequently asked

Is Buenos Aires expensive?
Buenos Aires is excellent value due to Argentina's currency situation. Using the 'blue dollar' (parallel exchange) gives effectively 2–3x the official rate. Even by official rate, it's affordable by Western standards. For US and European visitors it can be extraordinarily good value.
What is Buenos Aires famous for?
Tango (born in the conventillos of San Telmo and La Boca), world-class steak (asado), Boca Juniors and River Plate football rivalry, Recoleta Cemetery, the opera houses, and a café culture that rivals Paris.
What is the best neighbourhood to stay in Buenos Aires?
Palermo for the most options and energy. San Telmo for tango and historical atmosphere. Recoleta for elegance and safety. Villa Crespo for the best value close to Palermo.
