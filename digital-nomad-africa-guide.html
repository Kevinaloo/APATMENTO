Why Africa for digital nomads?

The African digital nomad moment has arrived. Fibre internet now covers every city on this list with speeds comparable to European capitals. Co-working spaces are proliferating. Nairobi's Nairobi Garage, Cape Town's Workshop17, Accra's Impact Hub. And the cost advantage is real: a month in Nairobi at the level you'd live in London costs one-fifth of London. A month in Cape Town at the same standard costs half of London.

Add year-round warmth, extraordinary food, extraordinary wildlife weekends and a genuine sense of being somewhere that matters, and the Africa digital nomad proposition becomes compelling.

1. Nairobi: Silicon Savannah

Nairobi is Africa's tech capital. The "Silicon Savannah" that houses the continent's most vibrant startup ecosystem. M-Pesa (the world's most successful mobile money platform) was invented here. Andela, Cellulant, Twiga Foods, mPharma and hundreds of startups are based here. The Microsoft Africa Development Centre is in Nairobi. Google's sub-Saharan Africa HQ is in Nairobi.

Internet: Fibre widely available; Safaricom Home Fibre covers most apartments in Westlands, Kilimani, Karen. 100Mbps plans from KES 3,500/month. Mobile data (Safaricom) is fast and affordable.

Co-working spaces: Nairobi Garage (Westlands, Karen), Wingu Hub, iHub, Jomo, Nest. Day passes from KES 1,500.

Cost of living: Westlands/Kilimani apartment: KES 25,000–60,000/month (furnished). Dinner out: KES 800–2,500. Grocery shop for a week: KES 3,000–6,000.

Visa: East Africa Tourist Visa (USD 100, covers Kenya/Uganda/Rwanda) allows 90 days. Kenyan Single Entry eVisa (USD 51) available online.

2. Cape Town: the most beautiful

Cape Town is widely regarded as the most beautiful city in Africa. Table Mountain, the Atlantic Seaboard, the Winelands, Boulders Beach penguins, and a food and coffee scene that genuinely rivals the world's best. It is also Africa's most developed digital nomad infrastructure.

Internet: Fibre widely available in Sea Point, Green Point, De Waterkant, City Bowl. Openserve and Vuma fibre; 100Mbps from ZAR 700/month.

Co-working spaces: Workshop17 (multiple locations), The Space, Hive, Bandwidth Barn, Springlab. Day passes from ZAR 200.

Cost of living: Sea Point/Woodstock 1-bed apartment: ZAR 8,000–18,000/month. Dinner out: ZAR 150–400. Excellent value relative to Western Europe.

Visa: 90-day visa-free for most Western passport holders. No remote work visa currently, but 90-day tourist stay is renewable.

3. Accra: the warmest welcome

Accra is the Year of Return destination. Ghana's government has actively positioned the country as a homecoming for the African diaspora, and this energy creates an extraordinary welcome for newcomers. The country is politically stable, English-speaking, and has fast-improving digital infrastructure.

Internet: MTN Ghana fibre expanding rapidly. 4G LTE is reliable for video calls throughout Accra. Co-working spaces have their own fibre lines as backup.

Co-working spaces: Impact Hub Accra, BuiD, Activam, Regus Airport Residential. Day passes from GHS 80.

Cost of living: East Legon 1-bed apartment: GHS 1,200–2,500/month. Dinner out: GHS 60–150. Lower cost than Nairobi or Cape Town.

Visa: Ghana offers visa on arrival for most nationalities. The Ghana Beyond the Return programme offers a 5-year multiple-entry visa for diaspora members.

4. Kigali: Africa's cleanest city

Kigali is the city that consistently surprises digital nomads. Immaculately clean, extraordinarily safe, fast internet, and the calmest environment on this list. Rwanda's government has invested heavily in becoming a tech hub. The Kigali Innovation City, the Rwanda ICT Chamber, and political stability create a genuinely favourable environment for remote workers.

Internet: Reliable 4G across Kigali; fibre expanding rapidly. MTN Rwanda and Airtel Rwanda both offer reliable data. Co-working spaces have dedicated fibre.

Co-working spaces: Norrsken Kigali (one of Africa's finest co-working spaces), Impact Hub Kigali, Hatch. Day passes from RWF 5,000.

Cost of living: Kimihurura/Nyarutarama 1-bed apartment: RWF 300,000–600,000/month. Lower than Nairobi or Cape Town.

Visa: 30-day visa on arrival for most nationalities (free for African Union passport holders). East Africa Tourist Visa covers Rwanda, Kenya and Uganda (USD 100).

5. Lagos: for the ambitious

Lagos is not for everyone. The traffic is legendary, the infrastructure can be challenging, and navigating a 24-million-person megacity requires patience. But Lagos also has Africa's most dynamic business ecosystem, the continent's most vibrant creative scene, and an energy that nowhere else in Africa (or arguably the world) can match. The food culture, the music, the art. Lagos is intoxicating.

Internet: Fibre in VI, Lekki and Ikoyi from Spectranet, Tizeti, Smile. Power outages are frequent. Good apartments have 24/7 generator backup, which is non-negotiable.

Co-working spaces: The Office Hub VI, Groveland VI, Co-Creation Hub (CcHub. Africa's most influential tech co-working), WeWork. Day passes from ₦5,000.

Cost of living: Lekki Phase 1 1-bed apartment: ₦200,000–400,000/month (furnished). More expensive than Nairobi or Accra but with Nigeria's extraordinary lifestyle to match.

Visa options for remote workers in Africa

  • Kenya eVisa: USD 51 single entry, 90 days. Apply at evisa.go.ke
  • East Africa Tourist Visa: USD 100, covers Kenya/Uganda/Rwanda simultaneously. Apply at any entry point.
  • Rwanda visa on arrival: 30 days free for African Union passports; small fee for others.
  • Ghana visa on arrival: USD 50–150 depending on nationality.
  • South Africa: 90 days visa-free for most Western nationalities. No digital nomad visa yet.
  • Nigeria: Visa required for most nationalities. Apply in advance at Nigerian embassy or online. Allow 2–3 weeks.

Pro tip: The East Africa Tourist Visa (Kenya + Uganda + Rwanda) gives you 90 days across three extraordinary countries for USD 100. Combined with Rwanda's gorilla treks and Uganda's chimpanzees and Nile, it is one of travel's great deals.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best city in Africa for digital nomads?
Nairobi is Africa's strongest tech ecosystem (Silicon Savannah) with fast internet and excellent co-working. Cape Town offers the most developed infrastructure and quality of life. Kigali is the safest and cleanest. All five cities on this list work well for digital nomads in 2026.
Is internet good enough in Africa for remote work?
Yes, in the cities covered here. Nairobi, Cape Town, Accra, Kigali and Lagos all have reliable fibre internet in established residential areas. Always confirm fibre availability before booking an apartment for longer stays. Co-working spaces have their own dedicated backup connections.
How much does it cost to live in Nairobi as a digital nomad?
A comfortable furnished 1-bedroom apartment in Westlands or Kilimani runs KES 25,000–50,000/month (USD 200–400). Eating out costs KES 500–2,000 per meal. Monthly total for comfortable living: KES 80,000–150,000 (USD 600–1,200). Dramatically cheaper than London, New York or Sydney at the same quality level.
Do I need a visa for digital nomad Africa?
Depends on nationality and country. Kenya: USD 51 eVisa (90 days). Rwanda: visa on arrival (free for AU passports). Ghana: visa on arrival (USD 50–150). South Africa: 90 days visa-free for most Western passports. Nigeria: advance visa required. Always check current requirements at each country's official immigration portal.
