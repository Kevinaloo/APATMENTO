One map, the whole product

Most travel sites give you a map of search results. This is a map of Cabana itself — every country, city and neighbourhood the platform reaches, with the categories that are live in each one. Click a pin and you get that place's real nightly range, its season, its currency, how people pay there, what a visitor needs to enter, and a direct link to every bookable page behind it.

Nothing on the map is decorative. Every pin is generated from a page that exists, so the globe cannot advertise a destination that goes nowhere. Where a place has live, bookable inventory in our database, the card says so and shows the real price range. Where it does not, it shows the typical range and says that is what it is. A travel map that guesses is worse than no map at all.

Fly it like a camera, not a scroll wheel

The camera pulls up, crosses the world at altitude, then descends onto its target — so a jump from London to Lagos reads as a journey rather than a cut. Arrivals end with a short focus beat, the same way a lens settles. Longer distances take longer, but only somewhat: the whole point is that the world feels big without the wait feeling long.

Six optics. Aurora for daylight, Ivory to read, Midnight for the globe after dark, Savannah for warm African light, and two ported straight from the open-source God's Eye View project — Noir and Nightglass. Press 1 through 6, or use the rail at the top of the map. The whole planet re-renders through the optic you chose, chrome included.

Six tours. The Grand Tour crosses every continent in half a minute. The East African Circuit runs Nairobi to Kilimani to Nakuru to the coast, the way guests actually travel it. The Diaspora Run flies the routes home — London, Paris and New York into West Africa. Press one and the camera takes over until you touch the map, at which point it hands control straight back.

The corridors are real. The animated arcs are great-circle paths between places Cabana serves at both ends — diaspora routes, trade corridors, safari circuits, domestic hops. They are the shape of how the world moves through this platform.

Share the exact view you are looking at

The camera position, the optic, the filters and the place you have selected all serialise into the address bar. Press the share control and you get a link that opens on your view — same place, same optics, same filters. Send someone Diani at dusk in Savannah, or the whole Southern Sweep, and that is what they open.

The world map: frequently asked

How many places does Cabana cover?
The map carries every destination that has a real page on Cabana — all 54 African countries, cities across Africa, Europe, Asia, the Americas and Oceania, and neighbourhood-level coverage in Nairobi, Lagos, Accra and Mombasa. The exact count is generated from the site itself, so the figure above the map is always current.
Does a pin mean I can book there right now?
A pin means Cabana covers that place and has a page for it. Where there is live, bookable inventory in our database, the place card says live stays with the real count and real price range, and the pin carries a green marker in the lists below. Everywhere else the card shows the typical nightly range for that market and labels it as typical. We never dress a catalogue entry up as availability.
What are the animated lines between cities?
Travel corridors — great-circle paths between two places Cabana serves at both ends. Purple is a diaspora route, blue a trade corridor, gold a safari circuit, teal a domestic hop. They fade out as you descend, because at city altitude a line across the screen no longer describes anything you can see. Press A to turn them off.
Why do some place names disappear as I zoom?
Because 175 labels at world zoom is a smear. The map runs a label allocator on every move: it ranks places by how much of Cabana sits in them — live inventory first, then how many categories they serve, then market size — and gives the top ones a slot that does not collide with any other. A place that loses its label keeps its dot and stays fully clickable. Press L to turn names off entirely.
Does the map work on a phone?
Yes, with everything intact. The tours become a horizontal shelf, the place card becomes a bottom sheet and the optics rail scrolls — nothing is removed. If you have reduced motion turned on, the camera stops animating and jumps directly instead, and the ambient movement stops.
What if the map does not load?
Every destination on the globe is also listed as a plain link further down this page, grouped by continent, and that list is generated from the same source the map reads. On a slow connection, with JavaScript off, or in a reader, the whole of Cabana is still one scroll away.
