Island or north coast?

Mombasa splits neatly in two. The island holds the history — Fort Jesus, Old Town's carved doors, the working port — and suits travellers who want character over beach. The short-stay market, though, lives on the north coast: Nyali, Bamburi and Shanzu, where modern apartment blocks and serviced flats line up behind the beaches, with malls and reef-protected swimming close by. Cross between the two over Nyali Bridge in minutes. Most first-timers should base in Nyali and visit the island.

What a night costs on the coast

Mombasa is gentler on the budget than Nairobi for what you get: studios typically run KES 2,500–4,500, one-bedrooms KES 3,000–6,500, and two-bedrooms KES 5,500–10,000, with true beachfront commanding the premium. December–March and July–October are the dry, busy seasons; the April–June long rains bring real discounts. Apatmento adds nothing on top of the host's price.

Getting there and getting around

From Nairobi it's about an hour by air to Moi International, or five to six hours on the SGR train — book the morning departure and arrive with the afternoon to spare. Locally, tuk-tuks and ride-hailing cover the north coast cheaply, and the Dongo Kundu bypass now links straight to the south coast without queuing for the Likoni ferry — Diani is an easy onward hop.

Good to know

Should I stay on Mombasa island or in Nyali?
Nyali and the north coast for beaches, modern apartments and malls — the best first-visit base. The island for Old Town character and history. Nyali Bridge puts them minutes apart.
How do I get to Mombasa from Nairobi?
Fly in about an hour to Moi International Airport, or take the SGR train — roughly five to six hours. Both run daily.
When is the best time to visit Mombasa?
December–March and July–October are dry season. April–June brings the long rains — and the best apartment discounts.
