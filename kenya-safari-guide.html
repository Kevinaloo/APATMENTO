Why Kenya for safari?

Kenya is the world's most accessible and most storied safari destination. The Masai Mara, Amboseli, Tsavo, Lake Nakuru, Samburu and Laikipia together create an unmatched range of habitats and wildlife experiences within a relatively small geographic area, most can be combined in a single 10-day trip from Nairobi.

Kenya's safari industry is 60+ years old. Local expertise. Guides, operators, conservancies. Is among the deepest in Africa. The country's private conservancy model (pioneered in the Masai Mara ecosystem) represents the most sophisticated integration of wildlife conservation, community benefit and tourism anywhere on the continent.

The best Kenya national parks and reserves

Masai Mara National Reserve

Kenya's flagship safari destination and one of the world's greatest wildlife spectacles. The Mara hosts the highest lion density in Africa, reliable cheetah and leopard sightings, large elephant herds, and the annual Great Wildebeest Migration (July–October). The adjacent private conservancies (Olare Motorogi, Naboisho, Mara North, Ol Kinyei) offer night drives, walking safaris and fewer vehicles.

Best for: Big cats, Great Migration, all-round game viewing. Best time: July–October (migration), January–March (dry, good predator activity).

Amboseli National Park

Africa's best destination for elephant photography. Amboseli's large elephant herds move freely across the open swamps beneath the backdrop of Mount Kilimanjaro. Africa's highest peak and one of the world's most iconic photographic compositions. Also excellent for lions and large bird diversity.

Best for: Elephants, Kilimanjaro views, photography. Best time: June–October and January–February (clear skies for Kilimanjaro views).

Lake Nakuru National Park

The most accessible of Kenya's major parks, 2 hours from Nairobi. Famous for white rhino (one of East Africa's largest populations), black rhino, lion, leopard and Rothschild's giraffe. The lake (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) attracts flamingos seasonally. Excellent for a long day trip or overnight from Nairobi.

Best for: Rhino, accessible day trip, flamingos (seasonal). Best time: Year-round.

Tsavo East & West National Parks

Kenya's largest park system. Combined Tsavo covers over 20,000 km², making it one of the world's largest protected areas. Famous for red elephants (they roll in red volcanic dust), large buffalo herds, Mzima Springs (crystal clear water, hippos and crocodiles), and the Lugard Falls. Less crowded than Masai Mara. A more raw, wilderness experience.

Best for: Wilderness, red elephants, less-crowded alternative. Best time: June–October.

Samburu National Reserve

Northern Kenya's extraordinary arid safari destination. Samburu has its own "special five". Species found here but not in the Masai Mara: Grevy's zebra, reticulated giraffe, Somali ostrich, Beisa oryx and gerenuk. The Ewaso Ng'iro River attracts concentrated wildlife. Remote and magnificent.

Best for: Northern specials, serious wildlife photographers, off-beaten track. Best time: June–October, January–February.

Ol Pejeta Conservancy

Africa's largest black rhino sanctuary and home to the world's last two northern white rhinos. Also has excellent lion, cheetah and elephant. The most concentrated rhino experience in East Africa. Based near Nanyuki, 2.5 hours from Nairobi.

Best for: Rhino (including the last northern white rhinos), intimate conservancy experience. Best time: Year-round.

What does a Kenya safari cost?

Kenya safari costs vary enormously depending on accommodation category, park choice and season:

  • Budget safari (camping, shared vehicle): USD 150–250/person/day all-inclusive. Public campsites in national parks.
  • Mid-range safari (tented camp, private room, shared game drives): USD 300–600/person/day fully inclusive of accommodation, all meals, and two game drives daily.
  • Premium / luxury safari (private conservancy lodge, private vehicle, exclusive activities): USD 600–2,000+/person/day.
  • Park entrance fees (Masai Mara): USD 200/person/day for non-residents. This is included in most package quotes.

Safari tips for first-timers

  • Book a private vehicle: in the Masai Mara especially, a private 4WD (rather than shared) lets your guide position you at kills and river crossings without being rushed. Worth the extra cost.
  • Use conservancies: private conservancies around the Masai Mara (Olare Motorogi, Naboisho) have fewer vehicles, allow off-road driving and night drives. More expensive, dramatically better experience.
  • Go in September: if you can only go once and want the Migration, late September is the peak. The river crossings are most dramatic, the weather is clear, and the Mara is at its most stunning.
  • Tip your guide and camp staff: safari guide tips of USD 10–20/person/day are standard and important income for local workers.
  • Book accommodation and flights together: charter seats to Mara airstrips are limited. Book early, especially for July–October travel.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Masai Mara safari cost?
A 3-night Masai Mara safari in a mid-range tented camp costs approximately USD 1,000–1,800/person all-inclusive (accommodation, all meals, twice-daily game drives, park fees). Budget options exist from USD 500/person; luxury lodges run USD 2,000–6,000/person for 3 nights. Book directly with local operators on Cabana at zero commission.
When is the best time for the Great Migration in Kenya?
The Mara River crossings. The most dramatic part of the Migration. Peak from late July through October. The highest concentration of wildebeest is typically in August and September. For calving season (equally dramatic predator activity), January–February in Amboseli/southern Mara is extraordinary.

How many days do I need for a Kenya safari?
Minimum 4 days (3 nights) in one park for meaningful game viewing. A comprehensive Kenya safari. Masai Mara plus Amboseli or Nakuru. Works well in 7–10 days. Add the coast (Diani Beach or Mombasa) for a 14-day Kenya trip.
