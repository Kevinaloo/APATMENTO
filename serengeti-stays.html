The Serengeti: earth's greatest wildlife spectacle

The Serengeti National Park (14,763 km²) is the largest protected area in the entire Africa migration ecosystem. The Great Wildebeest Migration is perpetual, over 1.5 million wildebeest and 400,000 zebra circle the ecosystem year-round following rain and grass.

Zones by season:
-January–March: Southern Serengeti (Ndutu). Calving season. Thousands of wildebeest calves and massive predator action.
-April–May: Long rains. Migration moves north and west. Fewer tourists.
-June–July: Western Corridor. Migration follows the Grumeti River. Grumeti crocodile crossings.
-August–October: Northern Serengeti (Lamai, Kogatende). Mara River crossings. Peak drama. Most expensive.
-November–December: Short rains. Migration returns south. Good photography, fewer tourists.

Beyond migration: The Serengeti has Africa's highest lion density, large leopard and cheetah populations, and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area (with the Crater) on its eastern boundary.

Getting to the Serengeti

Fly from Kilimanjaro Airport or Arusha to one of the Serengeti's 5 airstrips: Seronera (central), Grumeti (western), Lobo (northern), Kogatende (far north), Ndutu (south). Flights take 30–60 minutes from Arusha. By road from Arusha: 8–10 hours.

Serengeti: frequently asked

What is the Great Wildebeest Migration?
The annual movement of 1.5+ million wildebeest and 400,000 zebra through the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem. The largest mammal migration on earth. They follow rainfall patterns in a perpetual circle, with dramatic river crossings at the Mara River (July–October) and calving on the southern plains (January–March).
Is the Serengeti better than the Masai Mara?
Both are extraordinary. The Serengeti is vastly larger with more zones and seasonal variation. The Masai Mara has more concentrated river crossings in a smaller area. Many safari-goers visit both. The 'Northern Tanzania + Kenya' safari circuit.
What is Ndutu?
Ndutu is in the southern Serengeti, adjacent to the Ngorongoro Conservation Area. January–March is calving season. Tens of thousands of wildebeest calves are born here, making it Africa's most concentrated predator activity time.
