The lay of the beach

Everything in Diani hangs off one spine: Diani Beach Road, running parallel to the sand on Kenya's south coast. Apartments and villas sit either beachfront or a short walk behind it, with restaurants, dive shops and supermarkets strung along the road between them. Head south toward Galu for the steady wind that made this a kitesurfing destination; anywhere along the strip, colobus monkeys cross overhead on their rope bridges. It's a place you settle into, not one you commute around.

Prices and the season game

Season sets the price here more than anywhere in Kenya. In the dry high seasons (December to March and July to October), one-bedrooms typically run KES 4,500–9,000 a night and private villas KES 10,000–25,000 and up. Come in the April–June green season and the same keys often go for a fraction of that. Whatever the season, Apatmento's rule holds: the host's price is the final price.

Getting to Diani

Fastest: a light aircraft from Nairobi Wilson to Ukunda airstrip — about an hour, landing minutes from the beach. Overland, reach Mombasa by air or SGR, then drive south via the Dongo Kundu bypass, which now skips the old Likoni ferry queue entirely. On the strip itself, tuk-tuks and bodas handle everything.

Good to know

How do I get to Diani Beach?
Fly Nairobi Wilson to Ukunda airstrip in about an hour, or travel to Mombasa (flight or SGR) and drive south via the Dongo Kundu bypass — no more Likoni ferry queues.
When is the best time to visit Diani?
December–March and July–October are the dry high seasons. April–June is the green season: some rain, far fewer people, and the year's best villa prices.
How much does a Diani apartment cost per night?
In high season: roughly KES 4,500–9,000 for a one-bedroom and KES 10,000–25,000+ for villas; green-season rates drop sharply. Apatmento adds no fee on top of the host's price.
Is Diani walkable without a car?
Mostly, yes — life runs along Diani Beach Road, and tuk-tuks and bodas cover anything beyond walking range cheaply.
